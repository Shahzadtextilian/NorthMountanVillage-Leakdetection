Slab Leak Guide

Slab Leak Detection & Signs: Finding Leaks Under Concrete Foundations

By Leak Detection Pro Technical Team • October 3, 2026 • 7 min read

A slab leak is among the most feared plumbing issues a homeowner can encounter. Because water pipes run beneath reinforced concrete, an unnoticed sub-slab rupture can wash away foundation soil, crack tile floors, and inflict tens of thousands of dollars in structural damage. Here is everything you need to know about early detection and repair options.

What Causes a Slab Leak in Phoenix Homes?

In North Mountain Village and across Phoenix, residential homes are predominantly built on monolithic concrete slab-on-grade foundations. Several regional factors contribute to slab leak failures:

  • Copper Pipe Pinhole Corrosion: Phoenix municipal water contains hard minerals and treatment additives that react with copper pipe walls over 15–25 years, causing pit corrosion.
  • Friction & Concrete Abrasion: Copper expands when hot water passes through and contracts when cooling. If the pipe was not sleeved properly during original construction, rubbing against gravel or concrete wears a hole in the pipe wall.
  • Soil Settlement & Shifting: Expanding clay soils and seasonal monsoon moisture cycles cause concrete foundation slabs to flex, placing immense shear pressure on buried joints.
  • High Water Pressure: Excessive incoming municipal pressure (exceeding 80 PSI) stresses subterranean solder joints and pipe elbows.

Key Warning Signs of a Slab Leak

Because pipes are buried 4 to 12 inches beneath concrete, you won't always see surface puddles right away. Look for these hallmark symptoms:

  1. Hot Floor Spots: The most recognizable sign of a domestic hot water slab leak is a localized patch of warm tile, hardwood, or carpet beneath your feet.
  2. Continuous Sound of Rushing Water: When all indoor faucets, appliances, and hose bibs are shut off, faint sounds of running or hissing water persist beneath your floor.
  3. Unexplained Surge in Water or Gas Bills: A hot water slab leak constantly draws water and forces your water heater to run 24/7, causing gas or electric bills to spike alongside water charges.
  4. Damp, Darkened Baseboards & Mold: Moisture migrates upward through concrete via hydrostatic pressure, soaking drywall edges and closet carpets.
  5. Foundation & Tile Cracks: As subterranean soil washes out, unsupported concrete sinks, producing hairline cracks across floor tiles or outside stucco walls.
Warning: Never allow a contractor to randomly jackhammer concrete floors based on guesswork! Modern electronic leak detection locates the exact coordinates of the breach without widespread floor demolition.

Repair Options Once a Slab Leak is Located

Once the specialist identifies the exact point of failure, you typically have three repair solutions:

  • Spot Repair (Direct Slab Penetration): If the floor is accessible and the pipe is relatively new, a technician cuts a small surgical access hole in the concrete, replaces the damaged copper section, and seals it.
  • Overhead Pipe Reroute: For aging copper or delicate luxury flooring, the failed underground pipe is abandoned entirely. A new PEX water line is routed through interior walls or attic space.
  • Trenchless Pipe Relining (Epoxy Barrier): Where feasible, an epoxy barrier coating is pulled through the pipe interior to seal pinhole breaches without structural digging.

Frequently Asked Questions

How quickly should a slab leak be fixed?

Immediately. Running water will erode subterranean soil within days, creating underground voids that can cause concrete slab deflection, cracked floor tiles, and costly foundation settlement.

Does homeowner insurance pay for slab leak repair?

Typically, homeowner policies cover the detection costs and the access ("tear out and restore" of the concrete and flooring), while the plumbing pipe replacement itself is often paid out of pocket. Always consult your insurance adjuster.

Is a pipe reroute better than a spot repair?

If your home is over 20 years old and has experienced one slab leak, other underground sections are likely corroded. An overhead PEX reroute permanently eliminates future leaks under that concrete zone.
