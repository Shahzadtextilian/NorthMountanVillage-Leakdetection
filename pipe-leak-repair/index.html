Repair follows diagnosis

A durable repair always begins with non-invasive locating. If you have not pinpointed the leak origin yet, start with water leak detection, slab leak detection, or gas leak detection.

Modern pipe repair technologies & methods

Depending on whether your home features copper, galvanized steel, CPVC, or cast iron piping, independent contractors offer modern repair methodologies:

Surgical Repair

Precision Copper Brazing & ProPress

For accessible wall or floor leaks, specialists cut away the pinhole and crimp or silver-braze high-grade Type L copper fittings using flameless hydraulic press technology.

Non-Destructive

Overhead PEX-a Expansion Reroutes

Rather than excavating concrete floors for a slab leak, technicians abandon the leaking underground run and thread ultra-flexible, scale-resistant PEX-a piping through walls and attics.

Trenchless

CIPP Epoxy Relining & Soil Stabilization

For sewer, drain, or main line breaches, cured-in-place pipe (CIPP) liners create a smooth structural epoxy sleeve inside the old pipe without digging trenches across yards or driveways.

Important questions to ask your contractor

Before authorizing repairs, always request:

  • Their active Arizona Registrar of Contractors (ROC) license number.
  • A clear, written scope of work specifying parts, labor, and cleanup.
  • Whether municipal building permits are required for the scope of work.
  • Warranty coverage on workmanship and installed materials.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit for pipe repair in Phoenix?

Spot repairs generally do not require permits. Whole-home repiping or major gas line alterations require City of Phoenix permits and pressure tests, handled by your licensed contractor.

Is repairing or repiping better?

Spot repairs are best for isolated physical damage in newer lines. Repiping is recommended when pipes are older than 30–40 years or have suffered multiple pinholes due to water quality and wall thinning.
